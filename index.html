Case study

Worcester Park FC: a club site that keeps itself honest.

worcesterparkfc.com · 2026/27 season · visit the site

The brief

Worcester Park FC are a Surrey Premier County League club with a First Team and Reserves, a 1921 founding date and a trophy cabinet that got heavier in 2025/26. Their old presence was a scattering of social posts. They wanted one place that looked like a club with ambition and, crucially, never showed a stale table or a wrong kick-off time.

What I built

  • Live FA Full-Time data. League table, results, fixtures, postponements and player season totals are scraped from the FA's official pages into versioned JSON and rendered in the visitor's browser. Fixtures rotate off by date on their own, and FA's own feed takes precedence over any hand-kept list.
  • One page, two teams. A First / Reserves switch re-themes the whole site, navy and gold for the first team, yellow and navy for the reserves, with each side's own table, results, scorers and squad, and nothing from one leaking into the other.
  • Match Centre. Result cards open a dialog with everything the league published for that fixture, and only that: competition, half-time score, penalty shoot-outs, venue. Where the FA publishes nothing, the site says so rather than guessing.
  • Match-day content. Goal clips pinned to the results they came from, re-encoded for the web and paused when off-screen so five autoplaying loops stay smooth on a phone. A YouTube section with click-to-play embeds from the club's official channel. A club shop fed from the kit supplier's store.
  • Resilience against a hostile source. FA Full-Time sits behind a firewall that blocks most automated clients. The pipeline falls back page by page, never publishes an empty section over yesterday's good data, accepts browser-captured pages when nothing else is served, and reconciles derived figures against the FA's own totals before they go live.
  • Operations, not just launch. A scheduled refresh on the club's own machine commits data and redeploys. Squad spellings follow the FA register. Every change is checked in a headless browser at desktop and mobile widths before it ships.

Why it matters

A club site is judged on a Saturday evening by someone checking the score on their phone. This one answers in a second, matches the governing body's record, and still looks like somewhere a sponsor would want their logo.
